I was revisiting the OSI model recently. I'd studied it multiple times during my academic days, but somehow, it never really clicked.

I remembered the word ARPANET from my academic days, but that was about it. So, naturally, I opened my best friend, ChatGPT, and asked.

## October 29, 1969: The Internet's “LO” Moment

The story took me back to 1969, when researchers were working on ARPANET, short for *Advanced Research Projects Agency Network*.

Funded by the US government's research agency, ARPA, ARPANET aimed to connect computers at different universities and research institutions so they could communicate and share computing resources.

Back then, computers were expensive machines, often isolated from one another. Connecting them was a significant step towards the interconnected world we live in today.

On October 29, 1969, researchers at UCLA attempted to log into a computer at the Stanford Research Institute.

The intended message was simple:

`LOGIN`

But technology had other plans.

They managed to send just two letters:

**`LO`**

The receiving computer crashed before the remaining letters could be transmitted. The researchers later completed the login successfully.

And just like that, two little letters became part of networking history.

## From “LO” to Live Streaming

Let that sink in for a moment.

It's 2026. We make high-quality video calls, stream movies, play cloud games, deploy applications across the globe, and interact with AI systems in real time.

Yet, 57 years ago, researchers were celebrating the transmission of two letters between computers before the receiving system crashed.

Of course, the internet didn't evolve from that single moment alone. Decades of research, engineering, and collaboration made today's technology possible.

Still, it's incredible to think about how far we've come.
